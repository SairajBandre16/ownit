# Supporting your claims
> "Proves", "clearly shows", "significantly", "always", "the best": strong words need evidence.

## The rule
Every strong claim needs one of these in the same or the next sentence:
- a **citation** to a source: "(Rao et al., 2019)" or "[4]";
- a **number** from your results: "27% more energy";
- a **figure or table** that shows it: "(Fig. 3)".

If you have none of these, soften the claim: "suggests" instead of "proves", "in our tests" instead of "always".

## Words that need care
- "significantly" means statistically significant in science; use it only with a test result, otherwise say "by 12%".
- "proves": experiments support or contradict; they rarely prove.
- "the best": best by what measure, compared with what?

## Example
**Before:** The results clearly prove that tracking is always the best option.

**After:** In our three-day test, the tracking panel produced 27% more energy than the fixed one (Fig. 3).

## Try it
Search for "clearly", "significantly", "proves" and "best". For each, add the evidence or soften the word.

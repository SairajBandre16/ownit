# Word choice
> Prefer the common, exact word: "use" not "utilize", "start" not "commence", "about" not "with regard to".

## Why it matters
Long formal words don't make writing more academic; they make it slower. Examiners reward precision: the right technical term where one exists, and plain words everywhere else.

## How to fix it
- Keep technical terms exact: don't swap "torque" for "turning force" or "stress" for "pressure".
- Replace inflated general words: utilize → use, facilitate → help, commence → start, terminate → end, demonstrate → show.
- Avoid repeating the same word in back-to-back sentences unless it's a technical term (then repeating it is right).

## Example
**Before:** We utilized a multimeter to facilitate the measurement of the output voltage prior to the commencement of testing.

**After:** We measured the output voltage with a multimeter before testing.

## Try it
Find three words in your text you wouldn't say out loud to a classmate. Replace them.

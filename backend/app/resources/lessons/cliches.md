# Clichés
> "At the end of the day", "a game changer", "the tip of the iceberg": phrases so familiar that readers skip them.

## Why it matters
A cliché tells the reader you reached for the nearest phrase instead of the exact one. In a report it can also be vague to the point of being wrong: what exactly is "cutting-edge"?

## How to fix it
Ask what the cliché is trying to say, then say that:

- "a game changer" → what changed, and by how much?
- "state-of-the-art" → name the method or the year
- "at the end of the day" → "overall", or delete it
- "think outside the box" → describe the unusual idea

## Example
**Before:** This cutting-edge system is a game changer for farmers.

**After:** The system cut water use by 30% on a 1-acre plot, at a parts cost of ₹2,500.

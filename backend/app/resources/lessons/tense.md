# Tense in reports
> Methods and results are what you did: past tense. Theory and facts that are always true: present tense.

## Section by section
- **Introduction / Theory:** present for general truths ("A thermocouple produces a voltage …"); past for history ("Seebeck discovered the effect in 1821").
- **Methodology:** past ("The specimen was loaded at 2 mm/min").
- **Results:** past for what happened ("The output rose to 18 W"); present when you point at the report ("Table 2 shows …").
- **Discussion / Conclusion:** present for what the results mean ("This suggests that …"), past for what you did.

## Why it matters
Mixing tenses in one section makes it unclear whether you're describing your experiment or a general fact. That's a question an examiner will ask.

## Example
**Before (Methodology):** The frame was cut from aluminium. The motors are mounted on the frame and the data is logged every second.

**After:** The frame was cut from aluminium. The motors were mounted on the frame and the data was logged every second.

## Try it
Read your Methodology and underline each main verb. They should almost all be past tense.

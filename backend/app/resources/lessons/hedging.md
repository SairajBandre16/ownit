# Hedging
> "It could possibly be argued that this might suggest …" Hedge once, only where you really are unsure.

## Why it matters
Careful claims are good science. Stacked hedges ("may possibly", "might perhaps") make you sound unsure of your own results, and examiners will ask why.

## How to fix it
- Keep one hedge per claim: "may" or "possibly", not both.
- If you have evidence, state it plainly and cite the evidence.
- If you don't, say what would settle it: "A longer test would show whether …".

## Example
**Before:** It could perhaps be suggested that the lower COP might possibly be due to heat gain through the pipes.

**After:** The lower COP is probably due to heat gain through the uninsulated pipes; insulating them would test this.

## Try it
Search for "may", "might", "could", "possibly", "perhaps". Where two appear in one sentence, delete one.

# Punctuation
> Commas, colons and semicolons each do one job. Use them for that job only.

## The essentials
- **Comma after an introductory clause:** "When the soil is dry, the pump starts."
- **No comma between subject and verb:** "The output voltage of the regulator, was 5 V" is wrong.
- **Semicolon** joins two full sentences that belong together: "The first test failed; the relay overheated."
- **Colon** introduces a list or an explanation after a complete sentence: "We measured three values: voltage, current and temperature."
- **Space before units but not before %:** "5 V", "91%". (See the lesson on units.)

## Example
**Before:** The controller, reads the sensor every second and, if the value is low the pump starts.

**After:** The controller reads the sensor every second, and if the value is low, the pump starts.

## Try it
Find every semicolon in your text. Could each side stand as a sentence on its own? If not, use a comma.

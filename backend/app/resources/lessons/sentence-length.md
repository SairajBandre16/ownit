# Sentence length
> Long sentences make readers hold too much in their heads at once. Aim for most sentences under 25 words.

## Why it matters
A reader keeps the start of a sentence in memory until the verb and its object arrive. Past about 30 words, the subject, the conditions and the result blur together, and the reader rereads. In a lab report that costs marks for clarity; in a viva it means you can't explain your own sentence.

## How to fix it
- Split at "and", "but" or "which" when each side has its own subject and verb.
- Turn ", which …" into a new sentence starting "This …".
- Move background (dates, places, conditions) into a short sentence of its own.
- Keep one idea per sentence; list steps as steps.

## Example
**Before:** The specimen was mounted in the universal testing machine and loaded at 2 mm/min while the extensometer recorded the elongation, which was then used to calculate the strain at each load step.

**After:** The specimen was mounted in the universal testing machine and loaded at 2 mm/min. The extensometer recorded the elongation. We used it to calculate the strain at each load step.

## Try it
Find your longest sentence. Read it aloud. Where you take a breath, you can usually end the sentence.

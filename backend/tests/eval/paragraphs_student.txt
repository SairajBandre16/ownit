For our mini project we built a line follower robot using two IR sensors and an Arduino Uno. The first version kept losing the line on sharp turns, so we lowered the sensors by about 5 mm and reduced the base speed. After that it completed the test track in 42 seconds without leaving the line.
We tested the tensile strength of three mild steel samples on the UTM in the strength of materials lab. The average ultimate load was 48.6 kN and the samples necked clearly before breaking. One sample broke close to the grip, so its result is probably lower than it should be.
The main problem with our solar charger was heat. On a sunny afternoon the voltage regulator got so hot that it shut down after twenty minutes. We added a small aluminium heat sink and the charger then ran for over two hours without cutting out.
I chose a bridge rectifier instead of a centre-tapped design because it only needs a single secondary winding. The ripple on the output was still quite high, around 1.2 V, so I added a 2200 µF capacitor. This brought the ripple down to roughly 0.3 V at a 100 mA load.
During the survey practical our group measured the height of the library building with a theodolite. We took readings from two stations 30 m apart and got a height of 18.4 m. The value from the college drawings is 18.9 m, so our error was about 2.6 percent.
Our group designed a simple water level indicator for the hostel tank. It uses five probes at different heights and a ULN2003 driver to switch LEDs. The hardest part was stopping the probes from corroding, which we fixed by using stainless steel screws instead of copper wire.
In the heat transfer experiment we found that the finned tube lost heat much faster than the plain tube. At the same air velocity, the heat transfer rate was 38 W for the finned tube and 21 W for the plain one. I think the difference is mostly due to the larger surface area.
The first time we ran the motor with PWM control it made a loud whining noise. We found out that our PWM frequency was only 490 Hz, which is in the range people can hear. Changing it to 20 kHz removed the noise, but the MOSFET started to get warmer.
We did a load test on the single phase transformer and plotted efficiency against load. The efficiency was highest at about 75 percent of full load. At light loads the efficiency dropped a lot because the core losses stay the same while the output power is small.
For the concrete mix design we aimed for M25 grade. The cubes we cast were tested after 7 and 28 days. The 28 day strength came out at 27.8 MPa, which is slightly above the target, but two of the cubes had honeycombing on one face.
My internship was at a small company that makes control panels for water treatment plants. I mostly helped with wiring and testing PLC inputs. I learned that a clear wiring diagram saves a lot of time, because on the first panel we lost almost a day tracing one wrong connection.
We measured the flow rate through the venturimeter at five different valve openings. The coefficient of discharge we calculated was between 0.94 and 0.97. The last reading was a bit unreliable because the manometer fluid kept moving up and down.
The temperature sensor we first used was an LM35, but it gave noisy readings when the motor was running. We moved the sensor wires away from the motor cables and added a 0.1 µF capacitor at the input. The noise dropped from about ±2 °C to less than ±0.5 °C.
Our app lets students book lab slots from their phones. We used Firebase for the database because none of us had set up a server before. The booking clash problem took the longest to fix, and in the end we solved it with a simple transaction when a slot is booked.
In the lathe workshop I turned a stepped shaft from a 32 mm mild steel bar. The final diameters were within 0.05 mm of the drawing except for one step, which I cut 0.1 mm too small. I think this happened because I did not account for tool wear on the last pass.
We compared two filters for the ECG signal, a moving average filter and a Butterworth low-pass filter. The moving average was easy to code but it smoothed the QRS peaks too much. The Butterworth filter with a 40 Hz cut-off kept the peaks sharper while still removing most of the noise.
For the mechanics of solids assignment we calculated the deflection of a simply supported beam. The hand calculation gave 4.2 mm and the ANSYS model gave 4.5 mm. The difference is probably because the software also includes shear deformation.
The pump in our test rig was supposed to deliver 20 litres per minute but we only got about 14. We checked for leaks and then realised the suction pipe was too long and had two sharp bends. After shortening it the flow rate went up to 18 litres per minute.
Our team made a small wind turbine from PVC pipe blades and a stepper motor used as a generator. At a fan speed of about 6 m/s it produced 3.1 V with no load. When we connected a 100 Ω resistor the voltage dropped to 1.8 V, so the power output is very small.
I found the Bode plot difficult at first, especially drawing the phase. What helped was working out the phase contribution of each pole and zero separately and then adding them. Our plotted gain margin was 12 dB, which matched the MATLAB result.
We noticed that the soil samples from the site had very different moisture contents. The sample from near the drain had 31 percent moisture while the one from the higher ground had only 12 percent. This will matter later when we compare the shear strength results.
The Bluetooth module kept disconnecting when the robot was more than about 8 m away. We tried changing the baud rate but it didn't help. Moving the module to the top of the chassis, away from the battery pack, increased the working range to around 15 m.
For the surveying camp we had to prepare a contour map of a small hill. We used a grid of 10 m squares and took levels at every corner. Drawing the contours by interpolation took much longer than the fieldwork.
In the refrigeration lab, the COP we calculated from our readings was 2.4. The theoretical COP for the same temperatures is about 4.1. Our lower value is likely due to heat gain through the pipes and the fact that the compressor was quite old.
We programmed the ESP32 to send temperature readings to a web dashboard every 30 seconds. At first the dashboard froze after a few hours. The cause was a memory leak in our code where we kept creating new HTTP client objects instead of reusing one.
My part of the project was designing the gear train for the conveyor. We needed a reduction of about 20:1, so I used two stages of 4.5:1. The overall ratio is 20.25:1, which gives a belt speed very close to the 0.3 m/s we wanted.
The fatigue test specimens all failed at the fillet radius. This makes sense because the stress concentration is highest there. If we did the project again, I would use a larger fillet radius and polish the surface to delay crack initiation.
When we calibrated the load cell we hung known masses from 0 to 5 kg. The output was linear with an R² of 0.998. The small non-linearity near zero load was probably from the amplifier offset, which we then removed in the code.
Our traffic survey at the college gate showed the peak hour was 8:45 to 9:45 in the morning. During that hour we counted 1,240 two-wheelers and 310 cars. Most of the congestion came from vehicles stopping to drop students right at the gate.
We used a Raspberry Pi camera to read analog gauge values for our final-year project. Simple thresholding failed when the light changed, so we switched to detecting the needle with a Hough line transform. It now reads the gauge within about 2 percent of the true value.
I wrote the report introduction last because I only understood what our results meant after the testing. Looking back, our main finding was that the cheaper sensor was good enough for our purpose. The expensive one was more accurate but the extra accuracy was not needed.
